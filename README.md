## A future we can still shape

*The Hours We Keep* is an invitation to curate a vision together. Its appendix compares ten explicit indicators across two independent columns: high-level RFC-theme coverage and evidence already observable in public life. On the 13 October 2026 review, seven indicators have explicit conceptual coverage; two meet their real-world occurrence criteria, six have partial evidence and two remain unknown.

These are shares of a selected checklist, not percentages of the future, implementation progress or predictions. A data center beside homes is one documented resemblance; it does not make the story's political order inevitable. Proposals, working components and social outcomes remain distinct.

We can debate whether emerging capabilities concentrate decision-making in a technocracy, support a more just and identity-safe world, or lead to other arrangements. The practical questions are who can consent, refuse, earn, challenge and leave. No technology settles those questions by itself.

[^fiction]: This review edition uses Lowreach as a provisional city name. Its people, corporations and technologies are fictional.
